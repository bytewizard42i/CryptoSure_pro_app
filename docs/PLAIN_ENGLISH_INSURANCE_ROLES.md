# CryptoSure Insurance Roles in Plain English

## What does carrier-backed mean?

**Carrier-backed means an already licensed insurance company legally stands behind the
CryptoSure policy.**

Think of CryptoSure as the company that designs the specialized crypto-wallet protection
experience, security rules, evidence process, customer interface, and partner network.
The insurance carrier is the regulated company whose name appears on the policy, holds
the required financial reserves, accepts the insurance risk, and is legally responsible
for paying a valid covered claim.

### A simple example

Suppose a customer buys $1,000 of CryptoSure wallet protection:

1. CryptoSure explains the coverage and checks whether the wallet meets the approved
   security requirements.
2. A licensed producer presents or sells the policy.
3. The carrier issues the actual insurance contract.
4. CryptoSure may monitor the insured controls and organize claim evidence.
5. If a covered loss occurs, the carrier or its authorized claims administrator decides
   the claim under the policy.
6. The carrier funds an approved payment, even if the customer used CryptoSure's website
   throughout the experience.

CryptoSure can therefore be the brand and operating system for the product without
claiming to be the risk-bearing insurance company on day one.

## Why start this way?

Becoming a new insurance carrier requires substantial regulated capital, reserves,
actuarial work, financial reporting, governance, examinations, claims operations, and a
certificate of authority. Working with an existing carrier lets CryptoSure prove the
product and build loss data while the regulated insurer supplies the legal policy and
risk-bearing balance sheet.

## Who does what?

| Role | Plain-English meaning | Likely CryptoSure launch role |
|---|---|---|
| Carrier | The licensed company legally issuing the policy and bearing the insured risk | External partner at launch |
| Underwriter | The person or organization deciding which risks are acceptable and on what terms | Carrier, specialist underwriter, or delegated CryptoSure team later |
| Producer or agent | A licensed person or agency allowed to sell, solicit, or negotiate insurance | CryptoSure or a distribution partner after licensing |
| Broker | A licensed intermediary helping the customer or program find suitable insurance markets | Specialty broker helps CryptoSure find capacity |
| Surplus-lines broker | A specialist placing unusual risks with eligible non-admitted insurers | Possible launch channel if admitted carriers decline |
| Managing General Agent (MGA) | A specialist company with important authority delegated by a carrier, potentially including underwriting or claims functions | Possible later CryptoSure role |
| Reinsurer | An insurer that insures part of another insurer's risk | Possible capacity partner behind the carrier |
| Claims administrator | The authorized team investigating and administering claims | Carrier, specialist vendor, or delegated team |
| Forensic partner | A technical investigator tracing transactions and preserving evidence | CryptoSure partner, not automatically an insurer |

## What carrier-backed does not mean

- It does not mean the carrier has already agreed to work with CryptoSure.
- It does not mean every loss is covered.
- It does not guarantee recovery of stolen assets.
- It does not let CryptoSure sell insurance without producer and entity licensing when
  those licenses apply.
- It does not make a DemoLand price an approved insurance quote.
- It does not replace rate, form, sanctions, cybersecurity, claims, or state requirements.

## Pennsylvania starting point

CryptoSure's first regulatory conversation should use Pennsylvania's official
[Keystone Smart Launch program](../ref-docs/PA_KEYSTONE_SMART_LAUNCH.md). The program can
provide preliminary feedback on how the state classifies CryptoSure's proposed roles,
but it does not itself authorize the sale of insurance.

## Acronym decoder

- **Limited Liability Company (LLC):** A legal business structure that separates many
  company obligations from the owners personally.
- **Property and Casualty (P&C):** Insurance for property and for many kinds of legal
  responsibility or liability.
- **Managing General Agent (MGA):** A specialist company authorized by a carrier to
  perform important insurance functions within a written agreement.
- **Errors and Omissions (E&O):** Professional liability insurance for claims that a
  business's mistake, failure, or bad advice caused a loss.
- **Continuing Education (CE):** Required education completed after licensing to keep the
  license active.
- **Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZK proof):** A cryptographic method for proving a fact without
  revealing all of the underlying private information.
- **Service Organization Control 2 (SOC 2):** An independent report examining how a
  service organization protects information and operates security controls.
- **Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC):** The United States Treasury office that
  administers economic sanctions.

Documentation rule: write the full term first, place the acronym in parentheses, and add
a plain-English explanation when the term first matters.
