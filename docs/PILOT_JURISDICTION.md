# CryptoSure — Pilot Jurisdiction Analysis

> **Finding the best U.S. state to beta-test CryptoSure's privacy-preserving insurance model.**

> **July 20, 2026 update:** This document preserves the original jurisdiction research,
> but its West Virginia recommendation is superseded. West Virginia Code §33-60-2
> accepted sandbox applications only on or before December 31, 2025. No current extension
> has been identified. CryptoSure's active recommendation is a Pennsylvania-first,
> carrier-backed path using Keystone Smart Launch for preliminary regulatory review.
> See the [Keystone Smart Launch source record](../ref-docs/PA_KEYSTONE_SMART_LAUNCH.md)

## Original Executive Summary, retained for project history

After researching insurance regulations in Pennsylvania and all surrounding states
(DE, NJ, NY, OH, WV, MD), plus national leaders in insurtech-friendly regulation,
our recommendation is a **two-state strategy**:

1. **West Virginia** — primary pilot jurisdiction via its **statutory insurance
   regulatory sandbox** (WV Code §33-60). WV offers a formal no-action letter
   process, a 3-year beta test window, and a low $25,000 financial-stability
   threshold.
2. **Vermont** — domicile for a **sponsored captive insurance company** to back
   the pilot. Vermont is the #1 U.S. captive domicile with the lightest minimum
   capital requirement ($100K for a sponsored captive) and its own innovation
   waiver program.

Pennsylvania remains our **target market** for end-users (population, crypto
awareness, proximity), but WV is the regulatory entry point.

## State-by-State Analysis

### Pennsylvania (PA) — Target market, not pilot domicile

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Regulatory body | PA Insurance Department (PID) |
| Sandbox program | **None.** No statutory or regulatory sandbox. |
| Insurtech posture | PID issued AI guidance (Notice 2024-04, April 2024) — best practices for insurers using AI. Forward-looking but not a waiver program. |
| Crypto/digital asset stance | No specific crypto insurance framework. |
| Verdict | Good market to *sell into* once licensed, but no fast-path for an innovative pilot. Traditional licensing is slow and capital-intensive. |

### West Virginia (WV) — **Recommended pilot domicile**

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Regulatory body | WV Offices of the Insurance Commissioner |
| Sandbox program | **Yes — statutory.** WV Code Article 33-60 (Insurance Innovation), enacted 2021 (HB 2221). |
| How it works | Applicant submits application + $750 filing fee + technical white paper + innovation description. Commissioner has 60 days to accept/reject (deemed accepted if no action). On acceptance, commissioner issues a **limited no-action letter** — a safe harbor from specified regulatory requirements. |
| Beta test duration | **3 years** (with possible extension). |
| Financial stability | **$25,000 minimum** (commissioner can increase, decrease, or waive based on risk). |
| Consumer limits | Commissioner sets limits on number of consumers and coverage amounts. |
| Key restrictions | Cannot waive solvency/capital requirements, licensing/certificate requirements, taxes, or guaranty fund participation. The sandbox is for **product/process innovation** — not for dodging fundamental financial requirements. |
| Cross-state reciprocity | §33-60 explicitly allows the commissioner to enter agreements with other states' regulators to let sandbox-approved innovations operate across state lines. |
| Application deadline | Original statute set December 31, 2025. **Action item:** verify whether this has been extended (Kentucky extended theirs to 2030). |
| Verdict | **Strongest candidate.** Formal sandbox, low cost of entry, explicit blockchain/tech-friendly language (requires technical white paper — perfect for a ZK-proof-based product), and cross-state reciprocity provision. |

### Delaware (DE) — Traditional, no sandbox

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Regulatory body | DE Department of Insurance |
| Sandbox program | **None.** |
| Insurtech posture | No formal innovation program. Delaware is corporate-friendly (most US companies incorporate here) but its insurance regulation is traditional. |
| Verdict | Good for *incorporating* the operating entity, not for insurance regulatory relief. |

### New Jersey (NJ) — Innovation hub, not regulatory sandbox

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Regulatory body | NJ Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI) |
| Sandbox program | **None.** However, NJ launched **NJ FAST** (May 2024) — a strategic innovation center for fintech/insurtech startups in partnership with Plug and Play. This is a *business incubator*, not a regulatory waiver program. |
| Insurtech posture | Governor Murphy's administration is actively promoting fintech/insurtech. Good for partnerships and networking. |
| Verdict | Useful for business development and potential LP/partner relationships, but no regulatory fast-path for testing an insurance product. |

### New York (NY) — Heavy regulation, guidance-only

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Regulatory body | NY Department of Financial Services (DFS) |
| Sandbox program | **No true sandbox.** DFS launched "Project Whitehall" — a pilot program offering *informational meetings* and *guidance* to insurtech innovators. This is not a waiver/safe-harbor program. |
| Insurtech posture | DFS is engaged (AI circular letters, cybersecurity regulation 23 NYCRR Part 500) but the regulatory burden is the highest of any state. |
| Crypto stance | NYDFS BitLicense regime is stringent. Crypto-adjacent insurance would face dual scrutiny. |
| Verdict | **Avoid for pilot.** High cost, high scrutiny, no safe harbor. Enter NY only after the model is proven elsewhere. |

### Ohio (OH) — Emerging products office, no sandbox

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Regulatory body | OH Department of Insurance |
| Sandbox program | **None.** However, Ohio has an **Emerging Products and Innovation Initiative** that offers guidance and support to startups exploring innovative insurance products. |
| Insurtech posture | JobsOhio actively promotes Ohio as an insurtech hub. Columbus has a strong insurance industry presence. |
| Verdict | Worth engaging for guidance, but no formal regulatory relief mechanism. |

### Maryland (MD) — Interested, no formal program

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Regulatory body | MD Insurance Administration (MIA) |
| Sandbox program | **None.** |
| Insurtech posture | Commissioner Birrane (appointed 2020) expressed interest in attracting insurtech to Maryland. MIA has held roundtables on digital insurance. |
| Verdict | Sympathetic regulator but no statutory fast-path. |

## Beyond PA's Neighbors: National Leaders

### Vermont (VT) — **Recommended captive domicile**

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Regulatory body | VT Department of Financial Regulation (DFR) |
| Sandbox program | **Yes — innovation waiver** (8 V.S.A. § 15a). Licensed insurers can apply for waivers of specific regulatory requirements. Products must serve < 10,000 consumers; 12-month waiver with one 12-month extension. |
| Captive insurance | **#1 U.S. domicile** for captive insurance (600+ captives). Sponsored captive minimum capital: **$100,000**. Pure captive: $250,000. |
| Blockchain posture | Vermont has historically been blockchain-friendly (was an early state to explore blockchain for state records). |
| Verdict | Form a **sponsored captive** here to serve as the licensed insurer backing the WV sandbox beta test. The captive holds the capital, issues the policies, and the WV sandbox provides the regulatory safe harbor for the innovative product structure. |

### Kentucky (KY) — Sandbox extended to 2030

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Sandbox program | **Yes — statutory.** Extended through December 31, 2030. Allows beta-testing with regulatory exemptions. |
| Verdict | Viable alternative to WV if WV's application window has closed. Not a neighboring state but worth monitoring. |

### Wyoming — Crypto-forward, not neighboring

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Crypto stance | Wyoming allows insurers to hold digital assets in portfolios and recognizes DAOs as legal entities. Most crypto-friendly state. |
| Verdict | Interesting for long-term crypto-native insurance products, but not practical for a PA-area pilot. |

### Step-by-step:

1. **Form a sponsored captive in Vermont** ($100K minimum capital). This is our
   licensed insurer — "CryptoSure Captive, Inc." It satisfies the "licensed
   insurer" requirement that WV's sandbox implicitly expects (the sandbox waives
   *product* innovation barriers, not the fundamental requirement that someone
   stands behind the policies).

2. **Apply for WV insurance sandbox** ($750 filing fee + technical white paper
   describing the ZK-proof-based underwriting and claims process). The white
   paper is a natural fit — we already have architecture docs. Request a
   limited no-action letter waiving specific regulatory requirements that
   prevent parametric/ZK-proof-based insurance products.

3. **Sell policies to PA consumers** under the WV sandbox's consumer limits
   (set by commissioner). Use the cross-state reciprocity provision (§33-60-2)
   to negotiate with PID for PA participation.

4. **Run the 3-year beta test**, collect data, then apply for an extended
   no-action letter or transition to full licensing in PA.

## What the WV Sandbox Does NOT Waive

Important constraints from §33-60-2(b)(1):

- **Solvency/capital requirements** — the captive must be real and funded
- **Licensing/certificate of authority** — we still need a licensed insurer
  (the VT captive satisfies this)
- **Taxes and fees** — premium taxes still apply
- **Guaranty fund participation** — may be required after the beta test
- **Consumer protection** — commissioner sets minimum protections

The sandbox is designed for **product and process innovation** — exactly what
CryptoSure is doing with ZK-proof-based underwriting and claims. It is not a
free pass to operate without capital or consumer safeguards.

## Gaming Asset Pilot — Separate Track

The gaming asset insurance pilot (see
[`docs/GAMING_ASSET_INSURANCE.md`](GAMING_ASSET_INSURANCE.md)) may not need
the insurance sandbox at all if structured as a **service contract** or
**warranty** rather than traditional insurance. Many states regulate service
contracts more lightly than insurance. This is a separate regulatory analysis.
