# CryptoSure Pennsylvania Business, Licensing, and Underwriter Plan

**Prepared:** July 20, 2026
**Operating company:** EnterpriseZK Labs LLC, Pennsylvania
**Proposed brand:** CryptoSure
**Status:** Planning document, not legal, tax, actuarial, or insurance advice

## Executive recommendation

Launch CryptoSure as a technology-enabled insurance program, not as a new insurance carrier. EnterpriseZK Labs LLC should initially own the product, software, brand, security controls, and partner relationships. A licensed carrier or specialty insurer should issue the policy and hold regulated risk capital. CryptoSure can progress from technology provider, to licensed producer agency, to managing general agent only as the program earns data and carrier trust.

This is the most credible opening structure:

1. **EnterpriseZK Labs LLC** remains the operating and intellectual-property company.
2. Register **CryptoSure** as a Pennsylvania fictitious name if it will be used publicly instead of the LLC's legal name. Confirm name acceptability with the Pennsylvania Insurance Department before using it for a licensed insurance agency.
3. Ask the Pennsylvania Insurance Department for a confidential preliminary review through
   [**Keystone Smart Launch**](../ref-docs/PA_KEYSTONE_SMART_LAUNCH.md) before advertising
   real coverage, quoting prices, collecting premiums, or presenting DemoLand as a
   purchasable policy.
4. Form a carrier-backed program with a specialty broker, insurer, or Lloyd's market participant. Let the licensed risk-bearer issue the contract and approve the policy wording, rates, claims rules, distribution, and security requirements.
5. Build the first portfolio around small fixed limits of $500, $1,000, $5,000, and $10,000, strict eligibility controls, and a conservative aggregate exposure cap.

## Carrier-backed in plain English

Carrier-backed means that an already licensed insurance company legally issues the
policy, holds the required reserves, accepts the insurance risk, and is responsible for
paying a valid covered claim. CryptoSure can still design the wallet-protection product,
operate the customer experience, enforce security requirements, organize evidence, and
coordinate recovery partners.

The customer may experience the product through CryptoSure, but the licensed carrier's
name and legal promises appear in the actual policy. This lets CryptoSure prove its
specialized product without first becoming a fully capitalized insurance company.

See [`PLAIN_ENGLISH_INSURANCE_ROLES.md`](PLAIN_ENGLISH_INSURANCE_ROLES.md) for the roles
of the carrier, underwriter, producer, broker, Managing General Agent (MGA), reinsurer,
claims administrator, and forensic partner.

## The legal role ladder

### Stage 1: technology and program-development company

EnterpriseZK Labs develops the website, eligibility controls, evidence workflow, privacy technology, education, telemetry, and underwriter submission. DemoLand remains visibly simulated. No policy is quoted, bound, issued, or funded by EnterpriseZK Labs.

This stage still needs counsel review because compensation, customer solicitation, policy comparison, premium handling, and claims advocacy can each cross into regulated insurance activity.

### Stage 2: licensed Pennsylvania producer agency

The business entity becomes licensed to sell, solicit, or negotiate the carrier's product. The usual Pennsylvania sequence is:

1. Select at least one individual who will be the designated responsible licensed producer.
2. That individual completes the applicable Pennsylvania Property and Casualty exam process.
3. Apply for the resident individual producer license through Sircon or NIPR. Pennsylvania currently lists a $55 initial resident producer fee.
4. Complete required fingerprinting through IdentoGO after the exam and application, using the current Pennsylvania process.
5. Obtain Pennsylvania Insurance Department approval for the business-entity insurance name.
6. Apply for the resident business-entity producer license. Pennsylvania currently lists a $55 initial fee.
7. Complete insurer appointments or other carrier onboarding required for the product.
8. Maintain licenses and complete the required 24 hours of continuing education during each two-year cycle for applicable individual producers.

Confirm the exact line of authority and sequence with the Department and counsel before applying.

### Stage 3: surplus-lines distribution, if the admitted market will not write the risk

Crypto wallet coverage may begin in the excess-and-surplus market because it is specialized, novel, and lacks mature loss history. In Pennsylvania, a resident surplus-lines applicant generally needs Property and Casualty producer authority first. Pennsylvania currently lists a $200 initial fee for individual and business-entity surplus-lines licenses, and an individual applicant has an examination requirement.

Surplus-lines placement has state-specific diligence, disclosure, reporting, tax, and stamping requirements. It also generally lacks state guaranty-fund protection. CryptoSure should use a specialist wholesale or surplus-lines broker instead of improvising this workflow.

### Stage 4: Managing General Agent (MGA)

A Managing General Agent (MGA) is a specialist business that an insurance carrier
authorizes to perform important insurance work on the carrier's behalf. Depending on the
contract, that may include deciding which risks qualify, setting terms within approved
rules, issuing policies, collecting premiums, or managing claims. The carrier still
legally stands behind the policy.

An MGA relationship makes sense only after an insurer delegates material underwriting or claims authority. Pennsylvania's MGA framework requires a written insurer agreement and supporting appointment materials. The current Department instructions call for, among other items:

- an agent or agency license;
- insurer authorization and a detailed MGA contract;
- duties, lines of authority, and claims-settlement authority;
- a fidelity bond equal to at least 10 percent of the maximum annual premium volume, capped at $500,000;
- errors-and-omissions coverage of at least $1 million;
- organizational and ownership information;
- records and examination access for the Department.

Do not market CryptoSure as an MGA until counsel and the Department confirm the role and appointment.

### Stage 5: retained risk or captive, later

A captive, reciprocal, risk pool, or on-chain capital layer is a later-stage option, not a licensing shortcut. It requires credible loss data, governance, capital, reserving, reinsurance, audit, claims administration, investment rules, and regulator approval. Prove the product under third-party carrier paper before retaining underwriting risk.

## Data security and consumer protection

Pennsylvania's Insurance Data Security Act applies to covered licensees and requires a risk-based information-security program, corporate oversight, third-party service-provider controls, and cybersecurity-event reporting. Build for these controls before licensing:

- written information-security and incident-response programs;
- least-privilege access and multifactor authentication;
- encryption in transit and at rest;
- a data inventory, retention schedule, and deletion policy;
- vendor security reviews and contractual incident duties;
- secure software-development and independent penetration testing;
- immutable audit logs without putting personal or claim evidence on a public chain;
- a five-business-day maximum incident-notification workflow where the statutory criteria apply;
- sanctions screening at application, claim, payment, recovery, and beneficiary changes;
- consumer complaint, cancellation, adverse-action, privacy, and records-request procedures.

Zero-knowledge proofs can reduce data exposure, but they do not remove insurance, privacy, sanctions, or cybersecurity obligations.

## Underwriter attraction strategy

Underwriters do not need another crypto pitch. They need a controlled, measurable insurance program with explicit boundaries.

### The submission package

Create one secure data room containing:

1. a two-page program overview and requested capacity;
2. exact covered events, exclusions, conditions, waiting periods, deductibles, and valuation rules;
3. the four starting tiers and maximum aggregate exposure;
4. eligibility and wallet-security controls;
5. actuarial assumptions and sensitivity ranges;
6. claims, evidence, sanctions, fraud, and forensic-recovery procedures;
7. a recovery and subrogation waterfall;
8. privacy, cybersecurity, business-continuity, and vendor controls;
9. founder biographies, Alicia's finance-controls role, and external advisor roster;
10. a DemoLand walkthrough and sample bordereaux;
11. distribution assumptions and customer-acquisition evidence;
12. the requested commercial structure, commission, expense allowance, loss corridor, and stop-loss or reinsurance concept.

### The first capacity request

Ask for a narrow pilot, not an open-ended promise:

- Pennsylvania residents only, subject to counsel and carrier approval;
- $500 and $1,000 limits first;
- controlled invitation-only cohort;
- a fixed policy count and aggregate exposure cap;
- approved wallet types and security configurations;
- no private-key custody by CryptoSure;
- clearly defined hacks, approval-drainer, social-engineering, and user-error treatment;
- 30-to-90-day waiting period considered for anti-selection;
- monthly underwriting and claims reporting;
- carrier stop, audit, and rule-change rights;
- staged access to $5,000 and $10,000 limits after credibility thresholds are met.

### Initial outreach order

1. **Specialist wholesale broker or program-development broker:** refine the submission and identify live capacity.
2. **Evertas and its current carrier or Lloyd's relationships:** specialist digital-asset underwriting and risk expertise.
3. **Relm:** digital-asset and emerging-risk carrier conversation.
4. **Lloyd's coverholders and syndicates with prior wallet or specie experience:** market precedent exists, but appetite must be reconfirmed.
5. **Chainproof and comparable specialist program teams:** explore risk-engine and product collaboration while confirming who actually bears risk.
6. **Forensic partners such as Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic, and qualified recovery counsel:** evidence and recovery support, not insurance capacity unless separately confirmed.
7. **Reinsurers and alternative-capital providers:** only after a carrier sponsor and credible primary terms exist.

Always label each party accurately as carrier, syndicate, coverholder, broker, MGA, reinsurer, forensic vendor, or capital provider. Those roles are not interchangeable.

### Days 16 to 30: insurable product definition

- Freeze version 0.1 of coverage, exclusions, deductibles, valuation, waiting period, and recovery rights.
- Ask an ACAS or FCAS actuary to review frequency, severity, correlated-loss, and expense assumptions.
- Define wallet-security eligibility and the evidence required at enrollment and claim.

### Days 31 to 60: market submission

- Complete the secure underwriter data room.
- Hire or align with a specialty broker.
- Hold structured discovery meetings with the first five underwriting markets.
- Record every objection in an underwriting-issues register.
- Update the product only through controlled versioning so all markets see the same terms.
- Begin producer and entity-license steps if counsel confirms the role.

### Days 61 to 90: negotiate the pilot

- Select a preferred carrier and backup market.
- Negotiate capacity, commissions, reporting, claims authority, data rights, termination, runoff, and recovery economics.
- Complete security and vendor due diligence.
- Agree on rate and form filing or surplus-lines placement responsibilities.
- Turn DemoLand interest into a permissioned pilot waitlist, not a sale.
- Set launch gates for policy count, aggregate limit, loss ratio, fraud rate, recovery rate, complaint rate, and security incidents.

### Months 4 to 12: controlled launch and credibility

- Launch only after written carrier, licensing, filing, security, and operational approvals.
- Start at the two smallest tiers.
- Run monthly carrier reviews and quarterly actuarial reviews.
- Expand to $5,000 and $10,000 only when agreed credibility gates are satisfied.
- Begin SOC 2 readiness when the production control environment stabilizes.
- Evaluate MGA status only if the carrier wants to delegate the relevant authority.
